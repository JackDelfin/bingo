# 75-ball pattern catalog: definitions and sources

## Scope and provenance

The catalog contains **88 named presets** and **652 per-preset winning alternatives**, computed from the exported `PATTERNS` array. Alternative counts include intentional aliases across different named presets; they are not a count of globally unique masks. This is a broad convenience catalog, **not all possible bingo patterns**. Arbitrary house patterns belong in the custom pattern editor.

The New Hampshire Lottery Commission's *Approved Bingo Game Patterns* identifies established names including Blackout, Four Corners, Hardway Bingo, Postage Stamp, Double Postage Stamp, Six Pack, diamonds, frames, many letters, Butterfly, Heart, Christmas Tree, Hour Glass, Railroad Tracks, Layer Cake, Staircase, and Small Pyramid.[1] **The title of that source does not confer approval on this application, its glyphs, its callers, or its games.**

Arrow International's *Game Patterns* provides another primary reference for names and geometric conventions: straight lines, hardway lines, 4/6/8/9 blocks, corner stamps, multiple lines, crazy rotations, crosses, diamonds, frames, kites, and related silhouettes.[2] Its published simulations, proprietary page artwork, branding, probability/statistics tables, and payout material are **not copied** into this repository. The masks below are generated by original coordinate/union/rotation mathematics and explicit block-letter layouts.

Research checked on 2026-10-30. The NH document was successfully retrieved as extracted text; direct PDF download from this host returned HTTP 403, so its diagrams were **not** used to infer exact cell layouts. Arrow's PDF was downloaded and selected pages were rendered for visual inspection. A name in a source establishes that the named game exists; it does **not** establish that every house uses this exact mask.

## The data contract

- `BingoPattern` exports `id`, `name`, `category`, `description`, and `variants` with no UI dependency. `PATTERNS` is a `BingoPattern[]`; `getPattern(id)` returns that entry or `undefined`. The default id is `any-line`.
- Each variant is exactly **25 binary characters, row-major**: top-left is index 0, rows advance left-to-right, and the free center is index 12. `1` means required; `0` means not required, **not** prohibited. Extra marks do not invalidate a mask.
- **Any ONE variant wins** (OR between variants). All `1` cells **inside one variant** must be covered (AND within a mask). Double/triple lines and multi-stamp presets union their constituent shapes *inside* each alternative; they do not turn a single line/stamp into a win.
- Use the standard already-free center. Preserve its `1` when the selected shape includes it; do not erase it from a diagonal, T, X, filled block, or crossing shape. Hollow frames/diamonds and hardway shapes can legitimately have `0` at index 12.
- All drawn balls remain in play and counted. Some Arrow examples distinguish ignored-column calls or wild-ball play; those counting, wild-ball, progressive, and second-chance rules are **not** implemented by this catalog.[2]
- Rows/columns mentioned in descriptions are numbered 1–5. Rotation alternatives are clockwise quarter turns with symmetric duplicates removed. Unless stated otherwise, a single-mask letter/silhouette is fixed upright: rotation is not implicitly allowed.
- Ids `a` through `z` are the 26 upright block letters. `t` and `x` therefore also satisfy the explicit T/X APIs; no redundant `letter-t` or `letter-x` entry is needed. `full` is coverall/blackout, while `any-line` is ordinary straight-line bingo.

## Independently checked core geometry

Arrow p. 13, **Letter X All Calls**, visibly marks both complete diagonals, including the off-corner diagonal cells; p. 27 also shows **Letter X & Frame**.[2] Therefore `x` is the following **nine-cell** union, not the erroneous five-cell “corners plus center” shortcut:

```text
10001
01010
00100
01010
10001
```

Arrow p. 14 shows the upright **Letter T** as the whole top row and center column, and **Cross** as the whole middle row and center column; p. 27 shows each cross/frame or X/frame union.[2] `diag` intentionally means *either* diagonal, whereas `x` requires *both*.

## Naming and house-rule choices

These are explicit choices, not unsupported claims of universal shapes:

| Family | Catalog meaning | Source relationship / caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any line | 5 rows + 5 columns + 2 full diagonals | Standard straight-line family; Arrow lists 12 alternatives.[2] |
| Two / three lines | Unions of 2 / 3 distinct members of the 12-line set; 66 / 220 alternatives | Arrow also publishes other games called Double/Triple Bingo that permit corners or stamps. Those names must not silently change these **line-only** presets.[2] |
| Hardway | Exclude any shape that touches index 12 | Eight non-center full rows/columns; no diagonal qualifies. Hardway rectangles likewise exclude center placements.[2] |
| Postage stamps | 2×2 blocks in extreme corners only | Single=4 alternatives; double=6 pairs; triple=4 triples; four=one fixed union. General 2×2 blocks are a different preset.[2] |
| 4/6/8/9 blocks | Contiguous 2×2, 2×3 or 3×2, 2×4 or 4×2, and 3×3 rectangles | The 8-block is **not** the eight-cell inside frame. Geometry produces 16/24/16/9 placements.[2] |
| Frames / diamonds | Hollow and filled versions are separate, explicitly drawn below | A hollow shape does not gain a required center merely because “FREE” is printed there. Broken frame here omits four corners; other broken-frame gaps need a custom mask. |
| Letters A–Z | Original, explicit 5×5 block lettering | A complete alphabet is an application convenience, not a claim that every letter/glyph occurs or is approved in either reference. B/D/G/J/Q/R/V/W and similar glyphs can differ materially by house. |
| T / crazy T | Upright top-bar T versus any one of its four rotations | Arrow pp. 12 and 14 show crazy versus upright conventions.[2] |
| 7 | Full top row plus descending right-to-left diagonal | Arrow p. 13 shows the diagonal-stem seven; a vertical right-stem seven is a different house mask.[2] |
| Arrow / Christmas tree | Widths 1,3,5 in rows 1–3, then a two-cell center shaft/trunk | Both upright silhouettes occur on Arrow p. 17.[2] `arrow` adds four allowed rotations; `christmas-tree` is upright only. Their shared first mask is intentional. |
| Kite | Corner square plus diagonal tail to opposite corner | Small is a 2×2 square + tail; large is 3×3 + tail. Each has four rotation alternatives, not the union of all four. |
| Bow tie / hourglass | Horizontal filled side triangles versus the rotated vertical silhouette | Bow tie agrees with the rendered Arrow p. 19 convention; hourglass is the separately named vertical version. NH names both Bow Tie and Hour Glass.[1][2] |
| Butterfly / heart | Explicit house silhouettes drawn below | NH documents both names, but name-only evidence is not used to invent a claim that these are its exact diagrams.[1] |
| Railroad | Two **adjacent** full rows or columns, eight alternatives | Arrow p. 12 explicitly requires adjacency. Separated outside rails are a different house game.[2] |
| Layer cake | Full rows 1, 3, and 5 together | Visually checked against Arrow p. 24.[2] |
| Stair / pyramid | Thin nine-cell step path; small 1/3/5-cell triangle based at an edge | Stair is an explicit house staircase. Small pyramid follows the rendered Arrow p. 11 convention with four allowed rotations.[2] |
| Window pane / O | Window pane aliases cross+frame; square O aliases outside frame | Different named games may intentionally share a mask. Do not mistake such cross-preset aliases for duplicate variants within a preset. |
| Top/bottom half | Three rows including the middle row | Explicit house choice on an odd-height card; a two-row half must use a different mask. |
